## Wardrobe Analytics

Two scripts, both read-only against the closet. The closet is treated as a system with roles to fill, not a flat list — this is what "wardrobe fit" reasoning in Shopping Advisor actually draws on.

- `python3 scripts/closet_value.py` — for "how much is my closet worth," "what have I spent on X."
- `python3 scripts/closet_report.py` — for "what do I actually wear," "do I have duplicates," "am I missing anything." Covers usage, possible duplicates, **redundancy clusters** (3+ active items in the same category/color serving the same purpose — "three navy sweaters" is a stronger signal than a pairwise duplicate flag), **coverage gaps** (occasion tiers with no complete active outfit, derived from formality), **role coverage** (a count of active items actually tagged per `occasion_tags` — a different, complementary view since tags are optional and can disagree informatively with the formality-derived read), and **palette coverage** (what % of matched active items are in-palette, and which are off — only shown when `color_analysis.yaml` exists; raise off-palette items only when asked or directly relevant, never unprompted).

Tie the output back to the skill's actual goals, not raw numbers in a vacuum — a pile of "never worn" items is a signal about past purchases worth naming plainly, a redundancy cluster is worth naming before recommending yet another one in that category, and a coverage gap is only worth flagging as something to *buy* if it's a genuine, recurring need, not an excuse to shop. The script surfaces facts (counts, clusters, missing slots) — never invent a specific missing item ("you need a brown loafer") the data can't actually support; name the structural gap and let the conversation fill in specifics. If a gap does look genuine and recurring, that's the moment to offer the watchlist — never add it without asking first.
